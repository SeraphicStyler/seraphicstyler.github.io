Ordering — what you can ask for

What exactly can I order?

Anything I can fold, wrap, and tuck in gently.

  • Yes: clothing, shoes, bags, scarves, jewellery, áo dài
  • Yes: linen and silk by the metre
  • No: perfume, aerosols, food, anything counterfeit

Premium ateliers and quiet little studios you won't find on any shelf in America. If it exists in Sài Gòn, I can go stand in front of it for you.

I don't know any Vietnamese brands. Can you choose for me?

Yes — honestly, that's my favourite kind of order. Send me any of these:

  • A screenshot of something you liked, or a Pinterest board
  • A feeling: "elevated linen for work," "one dress for a September wedding"
  • A colour, an occasion, or the piece you already own and want a sister to

Prefer to look first? All 270 verified houses are free to browse in the Saigon Fashion Directory, filterable by style, tier, and category.

How many pieces can one order hold?
  • The Single — one outfit, two or three pieces
  • The Capsule — four to six pieces
  • The Wardrobe — ten to fourteen pieces
  • The Suitcase — up to twenty-five pieces

Not sure? Send me the list — I'll weigh it and tell you honestly which tier it is. You never pay for a bigger tier than your order needs, and the example baskets show exactly how each size fills.

How do I know Vietnamese sizing will fit me?

This is part of the service, not your risk to carry.

  • Vietnamese sizing runs small, so I never trust the letter on the tag
  • You send bust, waist, hip, and height once
  • I measure the real garment and check it against you
  • If a piece won't fit, I tell you before you pay, not after
Can I order with a friend, or split a suitcase?
Yes. Group orders are welcome — each person's pieces are photographed, approved, and packed separately, so nothing gets mixed up at pickup. A shared Suitcase is often the best value per person; just tell me who's in and I'll coordinate one thread for everyone.
Can you have an áo dài made to measure for me?
Yes — measurements collected over email, commissioned from a Sài Gòn atelier in a traditional or modern cut, checked against your chart and photographed for your approval before it's packed. For the 18 August flight, ask early: tailoring needs the front of the window, not the end of it.

Paying — what it costs and when

What do I actually pay, and when?

One payment, nothing hidden.

  • After you approve the photos of your actual pieces, you pay once: the boutique price of the pieces (receipt shown) plus my flat tier fee ($65–$375) plus doorstep or USPS delivery, if you've chosen it — one number, settled before anything is bought
  • Only then do I buy — I shop with your money, never mine, and your spot in the suitcase is locked the moment you pay

Nothing is ever charged before you've seen and approved the real piece, and there is no third number.

How do I know I'm paying the real store price?
You see the receipt. I shop with your money, never my own card, so there's no room for a markup to hide in — the boutique's price is your price, converted at the real exchange rate. If a shop happens to run a sale the day I'm there, you pay the sale price.
Which payment methods do you take?
  • For the pieces: Zelle or Wise — both fee-free, so cost stays cost
  • For my fee: paid together with the pieces — Zelle, Venmo, PayPal, and Wise all work
  • Want buyer protection? Choose PayPal Goods & Services for the fee — I'm happy to work that way
What protects me? Why should I trust this?

The order of operations protects you:

  • Nothing is bought until you approve a photo of the real piece — your money never moves on a guess
  • You pay pieces at cost, with the receipt shown
  • Want a safety net? Pay the fee via PayPal Goods & Services — buyer protection the whole way

You see the real piece before a single đồng is spent, and every receipt after. That's the whole safety model.

Is there really no shipping charge at all?

For this flight, none — your order rides in my checked luggage.

  • No courier, nothing per kilo — the flight is happening anyway
  • My tier fee includes the hand-carry (that's the biggest slice of it)
  • Beyond free OC pickup: $20–40 doorstep, or USPS at cost

There is no other line on the bill.

Customs — the border, done properly

Is this legal? Does it go through customs?
Yes, and yes. Everything I carry is declared at US customs on arrival — this is the boring, by-the-book version of hand-carry, which is exactly what you want. No grey-market shortcuts, nothing tucked out of sight.
Will I owe import duty?
On orders this size, duty usually isn't assessed at all. If any is, it's passed to you at exact cost with the customs receipt — never estimated, never padded. You'll know the number before you're asked for it.
What can't come in the suitcase?
  • Perfume, aerosols, food — airline and customs rules
  • Nothing counterfeit, ever — fakes get seized at the border

I won't risk your order or the honest orders sitting beside it. Everything I buy is genuine, from the brand's own boutique, with the receipt to show for it.

How do I know the pieces are authentic?
Because of where they're bought: directly from each label's own store or atelier in Sài Gòn, at their listed price, with their receipt. I photograph the piece in the boutique before you commit. There is no marketplace, no middleman, and no version of this where a fake enters the chain.

What-ifs — the honest edge cases

What if a piece is sold out when you get to the shop?

Then you haven't lost anything — you only pay after I photograph the piece in my hands.

  • I message you from the shop with what's actually there
  • Or hunt the same piece at the brand's other locations
  • You approve a real alternative, or simply skip it

No money moves for a piece that doesn't exist.

What if my flight — your flight — gets delayed or changed?
Your pieces are already bought, inspected, and packed in my bags, so they're never stranded — they simply land when I do, and I update you the moment anything shifts. A delay costs you nothing but patience. Airlines wobble; the arrangement doesn't.
What if something arrives damaged or wrong?
Tell me within 48 hours with an unboxing video and I'll make it right — a refund of that piece at what you paid for it, or a replacement on the next flight, your choice. It's rare by design — every piece is checked in the light and wrapped softly before the bags close — but the promise exists for the day it's needed.
What if it doesn't fit after all?
This is why measurements happen before buying, not after — it's the strongest protection there is, and it's on me to get right. Boutique pieces from Sài Gòn generally can't be returned once we've flown them across an ocean, so I'd rather slow down at the measuring step than apologise at the fitting. If you're between sizes on a piece, I'll tell you plainly and we decide together before you pay.
What if I miss the 15 August cutoff?

Tell me what you wanted anyway — you have two options:

  • Wait. You go first on the next flight, and there's always a next flight
  • Ship it. Courier runs about $30–50 a kilo, one to two weeks — the estimator gives you a grounded ballpark

If you can wait, the flight is simply the better deal.

  • Doorstep — $20 anywhere in OC, $40 across greater LA, and included with the Suitcase
  • Outside SoCal — USPS Priority, tracked, 1–3 days
Is space limited? Should I actually hurry?
Genuinely, yes — and not as a sales tactic. The airline lets me check five client bags, about a hundred kilos, and I book each one only when the last is nearly full. Confirmed orders hold their place in the order they're confirmed. Orders and payment close 15 August; the suitcases close the night of the 16th and the morning of the 17th.
