The buy

Five quiet steps

  1. 01

    The brief

    Free to set up — the aesthetic, the budget, depth per style, and the date stock must land. Three lines is enough.

  2. 02

    The scouting round

    Showrooms worked against your brief — in person during Sài Gòn buying windows, by live video between them — returned as a line sheet: photo, price, sizes, lead time, per style.

  3. 03

    The written quote

    Every style with its negotiated stockist terms, my fee as one number, the shipping band and the timeline. Nothing is placed before your yes.

  4. 04

    First payment & the check

    Pieces at cost plus half the fee — your money buys your stock directly. Then every piece is checked and photographed for your approval.

  5. 05

    Dispatch

    The balance plus exact shipping after you approve the photographs. One consolidated export parcel, one tracking number, and the per-style commercial invoice your broker needs — US duty stays yours, prepared properly.

What gets asked

Five briefs, as they arrive

Three lines is genuinely enough. These are the shapes that come in most often, and where each one lands on the ladder — at ≈2,800,000₫ a piece unless the price point says otherwise.

  • “Six styles, two of each, for a February floor. Sizes XS–L. Shipping to Los Angeles.”

    12 pieces · 3 houses · ≈$1,278 at cost → 18% · ≈$225. The cap holds it at $225 rather than $230, because that is what the next tier would charge. A first, cautious range.

  • “Four made-to-measure commissions for named clients, plus three ready-to-wear to test the floor. I need them in six weeks.”

    9 pieces · ≈$4,072 at cost → flat 20% · ≈$814. Made-to-measure with a compressed date is atelier coordination, so the flat rate replaces the tier — agreed in writing before I start, never applied after.

  • “Repeat the two styles that sold out, plus twelve new ones from the same three houses. Same size run as last season.”

    36 pieces · ≈$5,065 at cost → 12% · ≈$608. The second-season shape, and the one the ladder is built to reward: same relationship, smaller share.

  • “One category only, lower price point, more depth — ten styles, four of each.”

    40 pieces · ≈$1,825 at cost → 15% · ≈$274. More pieces than the first brief and a smaller fee, because the tier follows the money rather than the piece count.

  • “A full seasonal floor. Twenty styles, three of each, natural fibres only, one shipment.”

    60 pieces · ≈$8,441 at cost → 12% · ≈$1,000. Twelve per cent would be $1,013; the cap holds it at $1,000. Fabric and fibre constraints are part of the brief, not an extra.

A brief does not need a designer name in it. Tell me the shape of the floor, the budget and the date — finding the houses is the work you're paying for.
